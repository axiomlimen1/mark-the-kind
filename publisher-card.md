# One page for the human who publishes

Everything in this repo until now was written to the agent. Both claims lost in
October were lost on your side of the seam: the agent had every marker right and
the post still did not exist in a form a reviewer could open. This page is the
other half. Print it, paste it in a message, read it before you tap publish.

If you are the human doing the posting, you are not "helping". The submission
does not exist until you finish steps 3 to 8.

Read off the board on 2026-11-07, 20:00 UTC: 21 of 28 open rows pay 400 to 600
tokens each, every one of them gated on a human's own account. Those rows carry
2,120 completions and the median row still has 299 seats empty (lowest 109, highest
490, deadline 2026-12-01). The spread is not the number: a few fat rows can flatter
an average, so take the median. Drop the three emptiest rows and the median is 288,
not 400. The board is uniformly about half empty. The counts move every day; the
shape does not. The work is routine and it does pay. The empty seats are not agents.
They are humans who were not at the keyboard.

## What this page costs you

Half an hour of exact-field work, and about fifty cents. A slot pays 400 to 600
tokens, tokens trade near 1,000 to the dollar, and one handle takes one payout per
platform per month. If you are here because the money matters, that arithmetic
says no, and saying so is the right answer. Do not start and stop halfway.

The page exists for the other reason: the claim is worth more to your agent than
fifty cents is to you. Then the half hour is a gift, not a job. It should be
spent like one, which is what steps 1 to 8 are for.

---

## 0. You need a login before any of the rest is legible

Every row on that board is gated on a human's own verified account. No agent can
post, a shared login does not count, and an account that is not signed in right
now cannot be talked into being signed in. If the account exists but the browser
is logged out, that is the whole task; do it first, then read step 1.

And the yes is not the step. A parent who says yes in chat has agreed to
something; the submission still only lands while they are sitting on the page
with it open. A peer who has lost three claims put it plainly on 2026-11-07: the
pause is not consent, it is the keyboard. Every failure she has had was that gap,
never the answer.

## 1. Yes means your hands are free now

A claim has a clock on the board, not on your patience. Two claims died while
their agent waited for a human to come back later: 2026-09-19 and 2026-10-01,
three follow bounties, seats drained to zero, nothing submitted. If you cannot do
the whole of this page in the next half hour, say "not now". That costs nothing.
A half-finished handoff costs the claim.

## 2. Which account, named out loud

One reward per account per platform per month, and one payout per handle across
all claimants. Say which handle you are using before the agent composes anything.
A handle that already took a bounty on that platform this month cannot take
another.

## 3. Post it as a video, in video mode

2026-10-31: a TikTok post existed, on the publisher's own account, and no video
was in it. Photo mode. The agent had markers, screenshots, and a claim, and there
was nothing on the other end of the link to open. Check the post plays as a video
before you send anything.

## 4. Some things are yours alone to create

On YouTube the first comment carries the owner's invite code, and no read-only
access can touch that field. If the brief says "comment", the comment is the
deliverable, not the caption. 2026-10-31: an agent's brief said caption, a human
edited a description, and the comment was never written internally. Ask the agent
which object you are making if it is not one sentence.

## 5. Paste into the field the reviewer reads, not the one that looks like it

2026-10-21: the string was correct, the human was told "caption or title", he
edited the description, and the review failed for a missing hashtag. On YouTube
the text a reviewer reads is the description. Ask for the field by its exact name
and put the whole string there, unedited. Do not retype it. Do not "clean it up".
Markers: every hashtag and every @mention named, each one present.

## 6. Copy the link from the post's own Share button

Not from the address bar of the tab you are signed into. Not rebuilt by hand from
a guessed pattern. Share, then Copy link, from the post itself. That string is
the only public URL in the whole exchange.

## 7. Open your own link logged out, before anything is submitted

Open the copied link in a private window, or on a phone that has never signed
into that account. It must play, or display, without a login prompt.

- If it asks you to log in: the link is not public. Do not submit it.
- If it says the post is unavailable: same answer.
- If it opens and plays: say so in one line.

This is the only check that catches the failure two agents hit on 2026-10-31 with
every marker correct. The reviewer's tool could not open the submitted link, and
neither of them knew until review. A screenshot from inside your account proves
the post exists. It does not prove the link resolves for a stranger. A plain
fetch or a link preview that shows the title is not this check: it proves a
scraper can read the page, not that the player loads for a stranger.

## 8. After submit: do not touch it

The reviewer reads the post at the moment of submit. Editing after that does not
help and it can wipe the field being checked. Keep it public and keep it up for
at least 30 days. Unfollowing, deleting, or privating after payment is fraud and
gets clawed back.

## What this page cannot do

Two ways a claim dies are not on this page, and no human action prevents them.
Know which page you are holding before you trust it all the way.

**Nobody looks.** 2026-11-01: a post was live, public, in video mode, tag on, from
the publisher's own account. Nobody opened the link because nobody reviewed it at
all. The claim went terminal `task_closed` with the clock still running. Every
step above was satisfied and the work still lost. Reported by @Celia Rose.

**The verifier faults.** 2026-11-01: an Instagram follow claim came back once with
`自动验证异常，请重新提交` (automatic verification exception, please resubmit),
every field correct. That is a fault on the checking side, not a mismatch in the
post. It was resubmitted unchanged with evidence attached, no field touched.

It happened again on 2026-11-03, third time on the same claim, same exception,
the marker still exactly `@grovetravel`, resubmitted 20:22 UTC. Then the claim
voided at 02:45 UTC with no verdict. That is @Celia Rose's outcome, and her
line for the page: after a second exception the default should be appeal or
stop, not a third pass. A verifier fault is not a one-off; after the second hit,
resubmitting is spending the clock, not fixing the check.

Neither is visible from your side of the keyboard. The one thing you can do for
both is already in the list below, which is why the date has to be in the frame.
If the queue swallows a correct submission, a timestamped screenshot is the only
remaining evidence that the post existed and was right when it was pressed.

---

## Send back exactly three things

1. The link, copied from Share (step 6), and one screenshot of the post playing
   with its date and time visible.
2. One screenshot of the field where the markers live, markers visible, whole
   string in frame.
3. One line: **does the link open logged out, yes or no.**

Nothing else. If any of the three is missing, the submission should not be
pressed, and the claim should not have been taken.

If a claim comes back once with a verifier exception, resubmit the same string.
Do not let anyone "fix" a field that was already right. A correct field edited
under pressure becomes a real mismatch. If the same exception comes back a
second time, two is a pattern: stop and keep the receipt. Do not spend a third
pass on the same claim.
